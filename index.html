Onsurity — Homepage Redesign

Onsurity sells monthly healthcare memberships to small businesses. Over time its app homepage had filled up with banners and nudges, and the things people opened the app for were the hardest to find. We changed that.

Two-designer team · in collaboration with Satyam Srivastava

Year
2023
Timeline
4 weeks to handoff
Team
2 designers · PM · Android & iOS
Reach
~5,000 daily users

The problem

The most important actions were the hardest to find

Promo banners, swipe menus and nudges had piled up on the homepage over time. Members who wanted to raise a claim, find a cashless hospital or show their health card had to scroll past marketing to reach them, often while dealing with a medical problem.

Evidence

Three sources, one answer

  • UX auditWe mapped every route on the homepage. The top user tasks were buried the deepest.
  • Support ticketsMany tickets were simply people asking where to find a feature.
  • App store reviewsFrustration about claims kept coming up.

Prioritisation

16 opportunities, scored on impact against effort

We scored every opportunity on user impact and build effort. Phase 1 took everything with high impact that didn't need backend changes.

Impact ↑ Effort →

The highlighted top-left area is Phase 1: high impact with no backend rework.

  • Critical actions on topClaims, cashless hospitals and policy cards come first
  • Insurance and Wellness separatedTwo clear sections instead of one mixed feed
  • Active plans separate from upsellsWhat you have is kept apart from what you could buy
  • Profile as home baseMember details and claim history live in one place
  • Upcoming orders shownAppointments and orders are visible on the homepage
  • Support restructuredFAQs answer simple questions before they become tickets

The key decision

Three wireframes, three stakeholders

Each layout represented a different view inside the company. We tested all three against the support data.

A · Promo firstMarketing's preference

B · Wellness feed firstThe founder's instinct

C · Critical actions first✓ Chosen, backed by support data

The trade-off: We expected fewer promo impressions. We accepted that, because members who can quickly find help when they're unwell are more likely to stay.

Visual approach

Working inside the existing brand

A planned new illustration system was cancelled for business reasons, so the redesign stayed close to the existing brand. We added illustrations only to the action cards, where they help people understand what each card does at a glance.

Results

What we're measuring

It's too early for conclusive numbers. We're tracking:

  • Navigation ticketsThe number of "where do I find…" tickets
  • First-screen engagementTaps on the critical action cards
  • Order visibilityWhether people can see upcoming appointments
  • Store ratingsChanges in claim-related reviews

The structural win: new products can now launch on the homepage without another redesign.

Reflection

What I'd do differently

I would make the illustration system a formal requirement for the homepage, not a parallel track. If it was going to be cancelled, that should have been a visible decision rather than something the team simply absorbed.
